# Gospel of Truth

*A Modern Contemplative Reading*

This repository contains a modern contemplative reading of the **Gospel of Truth**, an early Christian work associated with Valentinian Christianity and preserved among the texts discovered at Nag Hammadi.

Rather than presenting a new translation or scholarly commentary, this project reimagines the *Gospel of Truth* as **30 daily contemplative readings**. Each entry draws from a portion of the ancient text and develops its imagery in accessible devotional language for a modern reader.

The goal is not to remove the strangeness of the original work or make Valentinian Christianity conform neatly to contemporary theology. Instead, these readings listen for the spiritual movement within the text: from ignorance to knowledge, forgetfulness to remembrance, fear to love, division to wholeness, wandering to return, and finally rest in the Father.

## About the Gospel of Truth

The *Gospel of Truth* is generally dated to the second century and is commonly associated with the Valentinian Christian tradition. Although called a "gospel," it is not a narrative of the life of Jesus like the canonical Gospels. It reads more like a sermon or theological meditation.

Its language can initially feel unfamiliar. It speaks of the **Pleroma**, deficiency, Error, knowledge, forgetfulness, and the relationship between the Father and the Son. Yet beneath this ancient cosmological language is a deeply contemplative vision of the human condition.

Humanity has forgotten its Source.

From that forgetfulness arise fear, confusion, division, and suffering. Christ comes as the revelation of the Father, awakening those who sleep, illuminating those who wander, healing what is wounded, and leading creation back toward its fullness.

## The Father at the Center

One theme became especially important while developing these readings: **the goodness of the Father**.

Christian theology has sometimes left people with an image of Jesus as the compassionate Son protecting humanity from an angry Father. The *Gospel of Truth* offers a different movement. The Son comes from the Father's heart and reveals what has always been there.

The compassion of Jesus is therefore not a correction to the character of God. It is the character of God made visible.

If Jesus searches for the lost, the Father searches for the lost.

If Jesus moves toward the wounded, the Father moves toward the wounded.

If Jesus speaks mercy, that mercy has its source in the Father.

**If you can trust Jesus, you can trust the Father.**

Within these readings, salvation therefore becomes less about escaping divine anger and more about awakening to divine love. The Father is not the enemy standing at the end of the spiritual journey. The Father is home.

## Structure

The book is organized as thirty daily readings following the movement of the original text.

### Part One — Forgetting and Being Called

Days 1–10 explore ignorance, fear, forgetfulness, and the first movements of awakening.

1. The Joy of Knowing
2. The Fog of Ignorance
3. When Forgetfulness Ends
4. A Path Through Darkness
5. The Fruit of the Tree
6. What the All Lacked
7. The Quiet Teacher
8. The Living Book
9. What Belongs to Us
10. Called by Name

### Part Two — Knowing and Awakening

Days 11–20 move deeper into revelation, identity, healing, and the recovery of what has been lost.

11. The Letters of Truth
12. The Father's Open Heart
13. From Division to Unity
14. The House Made Whole
15. When Truth Appears
16. Given Form and Name
17. The Nightmare
18. Awakening
19. The Path for Those Who Wander
20. The Lost Sheep

### Part Three — Returning and Resting

Days 21–30 follow the movement outward into compassion and inward toward restoration, fullness, and rest in the Father.

21. The Sabbath Without Night
22. Awaken Those Who Sleep
23. Do the Will of the Father
24. The Fragrance of the Father
25. Turning Back
26. The Physician Comes
27. The Place of Rest
28. The Will of the Father
29. The Name
30. Returning to the Father

## Reading the Book

These entries are intended to be read slowly.

Each begins with a short contemplative phrase, followed by a reflection drawn from the themes and imagery of the corresponding passage of the *Gospel of Truth*. Each reading concludes with a simple breath prayer that can be used as a point of meditation or carried into the rest of the day.

There is no need to understand Valentinian cosmology before beginning. Readers interested in the historical and theological background may certainly explore it further, but this project is primarily devotional rather than academic.

Read one entry each day.

Sit with the images.

Notice what unsettles you and what gives you hope.

And perhaps allow some old images of God to be reconsidered along the way.

## A Modern Contemplative Reading

This work should not be treated as a translation of the *Gospel of Truth*.

The readings expand, interpret, and sometimes translate ancient theological images into contemporary contemplative language. They are intended to remain in conversation with the original text while allowing that text to speak within a different time, culture, and spiritual vocabulary.

Readers interested in studying the historical *Gospel of Truth* should consult scholarly translations and academic treatments of the Valentinian tradition alongside this work.

This project is instead an invitation to listen.

To remember.

To awaken.

And ultimately, to return.

> You are returning to the Love from which you came.
