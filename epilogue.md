## Epilogue

We began with joy.

That may be easy to forget after traveling through the strange landscape of the *Gospel of Truth*. We have passed through fog and forgetfulness, encountered Error and deficiency, wandered through nightmares, watched the lost sheep carried home, and followed the physician into the places that hurt. Yet beneath all these images has been a remarkably simple movement: something that has forgotten where it came from is remembering how to return.

Perhaps that is why the image of home has stayed with me.

So much religious language begins with separation. God is there and we are here. Something has gone terribly wrong between us, and the task of salvation is somehow to repair the breach. Depending upon the theology we inherited, we may have learned to imagine God waiting on the other side of that distance with disappointment, judgment, or anger.

But the Father we have encountered in these pages has been difficult to fit into that picture.

This Father knows our names before we know ourselves. His heart opens toward us. His fragrance clings to creation, his fullness moves toward our deficiency, and his physician hurries toward the place where we are sick. When one sheep is missing, he does not calculate that ninety-nine are sufficient. Love notices the empty place and goes searching.

And then there is Jesus.

Perhaps one of the most healing things Christianity can recover is the simple conviction that Jesus tells us what God is like. His compassion is not a correction to the Father's severity. His mercy is not something he must persuade the Father to share. He does not stand between us and an unwilling God.

He comes from the Father's heart.

The hands that touch the unclean reveal the Father. The voice that forgives reveals the Father. The shepherd searching through the wilderness reveals the Father, and the physician bending over the wounded reveals the Father. When Jesus refuses to abandon those whom others have condemned, we are not watching the Son argue with the character of God; we are watching the character of God become visible.

The words of love we hear from Jesus were first alive in the heart of the Father.

If you can trust Jesus, you can trust the Father.

For some of us, that may take time. Images of God formed through fear do not disappear simply because we replace them with better sentences. They can settle deep within us, shaping the way we pray, the way we interpret suffering, and even the way we speak to ourselves when we fail.

Perhaps those images do not need to be torn away violently. They can simply be brought into the light, where we can ask whether they resemble the face we have seen in Christ. The God who frightens us can slowly give way to the Father who opens his heart.

This does not remove mystery. God remains beyond every name, doctrine, metaphor, and theological system we construct. The *Gospel of Truth* itself reaches repeatedly toward something it cannot quite contain in words, piling image upon image because no single one is enough.

But mystery need not mean menace.

Perhaps the mystery at the heart of existence is goodness deeper than we can understand. Perhaps the One beyond every thought is not less loving than the best love we have known, but infinitely more. Perhaps the sweetness we occasionally taste in this life is not an exception within a cold universe, but a fragrance carried from its Source.

Then salvation begins to look different.

It becomes awakening from the nightmare. It becomes remembering what fear caused us to forget, allowing what has been divided to become whole, and turning toward the light when we discover ourselves wandering. It becomes letting the physician enter the wound and allowing the shepherd to carry home what we could not rescue ourselves.

And eventually, it becomes rest.

Not the rest of having solved every theological question or finally become the person we think we ought to be. It is the rest of no longer believing that we must persuade God to love us. It is the quiet recognition that our lives began within Love, have been accompanied by Love, and are being drawn toward Love still.

The journey of these thirty readings ends where it began. The seeker discovers that the One being sought was never absent. The lost discovers that the shepherd was already searching, and the frightened child discovers that the voice calling through the darkness knows their name.

There may still be much we do not understand. There will still be wandering, forgetting, and mornings when the fog settles again. But perhaps now we know something we can remember when it does.

The Father is not the darkness.

The Father is home.

And the One waiting there is not bitter, distant, or angry, but good, unperturbed, and sweet. We have come from this Love, and toward this Love we are returning.

We are children of this Love.

And we are loved.
