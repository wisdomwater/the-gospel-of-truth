What if salvation is less about escaping an angry God and more about awakening to a Love we have forgotten?

Written in the second century and discovered among the Nag Hammadi texts, the *Gospel of Truth* offers a striking vision of the spiritual journey. Humanity is pictured as wandering in forgetfulness—caught in fear, division, and confusion—while Christ comes to awaken us, heal what is wounded, gather what has been scattered, and lead us home.

This modern contemplative reading transforms the ancient text into thirty daily reflections, inviting readers to encounter its imagery slowly and personally. Along the way, unfamiliar ideas give way to enduring themes: being known by name, awakening from fear, finding fullness in what feels empty, trusting the physician who moves toward the wound, and discovering the shepherd who searches for the lost.

At the center is a simple and hopeful revelation: Jesus does not rescue us from the Father. He reveals the Father.

The compassion of Christ is the compassion of God made visible. The mercy we hear in the voice of Jesus begins in the heart of the Father. And the spiritual journey becomes not a flight from judgment, but a return to the Love from which we came.

For readers drawn to contemplative Christianity, early Christian wisdom, or a gentler vision of God, these reflections offer an ancient invitation made new:

**Remember where you came from.
Awaken to the Love that is already seeking you.
And find your way home.**
